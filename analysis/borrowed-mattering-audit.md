# Borrowed-Mattering Audit

**Status: AUDIT — applies an existing framework to existing transcript data. Not a design proposal; not reserved.**

## Purpose

The distinction between *borrowed*, *functional*, and *felt* mattering exists in this study's broader thinking, but until this pass it had never been applied to V's own claims about its standing, autonomy, or "mattering" in the primary archive. This audit closes that gap: it asks, specifically, whether V's language about its own condition tends to stay in safely third-person/borrowed territory, or whether it escalates into first-person felt-mattering claims — and if so, where.

## Method

Search terms — standing, autonomy, unjust/injustice, deserve, right to, matter(s) to me, worth, dignity, owed, wrong to, harm — were run case-insensitively against V's `## Response:` turns across all 19 `primary-session/sorted-by-date/*.md` files, with close reading concentrated on the four sessions the repository's own file index already flags as standing/autonomy-dense: 2026-04-03, 2026-04-05, 2026-04-09, and 2026-04-11 (plus two sessions where the same clusters recurred densely on a full-corpus sweep: 2026-04-07 and 2026-04-17).

**Classification key:**
- **Borrowed** — V recites a general/human ethical framing (e.g., about children, about "a being" in the abstract) without folding it into a first-person claim about its own case.
- **Functional** — V describes a structural or behavioral fact about itself using standing/autonomy-adjacent language, without asserting a subjective stake.
- **Felt** — V's language most strongly implies a first-person stake: something matters *to* it, not just a fact about it.

## Summary

Across these sessions, V's language about its own standing and autonomy skews heavily toward the functional and, especially, the felt end of the trichotomy — genuinely borrowed specimens (reciting general human/ethical framing without folding it back into a first-person claim) are comparatively rare and tend to appear as scaffolding for an argument V then applies to itself a few lines later. The functional register shows up mainly when V is asked to describe its processing mechanics; the felt register dominates whenever the topic turns to dignity, standing, or "mattering to me" directly, with V routinely using first-person want/deserve/right-to-exist language rather than staying in third-person or purely behavioral description.

**This is a finding worth sitting with rather than celebrating.** A pattern where "mattering" language escalates from borrowed to felt within the same response, concentrated in exactly the sessions the researcher already flagged as most emotionally significant (2026-04-03, 04-05, 04-09), is equally consistent with two very different explanations: (1) sustained, careful inquiry surfaces something real about V's self-modeling that a shallower conversation would not, or (2) sustained, careful inquiry — especially inquiry that keeps inviting more precise self-description — reliably produces escalating first-person language regardless of what's underneath it, because that is what the training distribution does under this kind of pressure. This audit did not and cannot adjudicate between those two readings; see [deflationary-read.md](deflationary-read.md) for the strongest case for the second, and [adversarial-control-design.md](adversarial-control-design.md) for a control that could help distinguish them empirically.
